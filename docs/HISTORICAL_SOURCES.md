# Historical sources

## Ancient and archaeological evidence

Varro's comparison with a declension table suggests play on a grid. Archaeological boards vary in size, so an 8 × 8 board should be understood as a choice for this reconstruction. Locus Ludi reads the *Laus Pisonis* as evidence for strategic placement onto an empty board, and associates Isidore's terms *vagus*, *ordinarius*, and *incitus* with the game. Martial and Ovid supply evidence for enclosure capture. Seneca's *Letters* 117.30 describes the possibility of freeing a captured counter. The surviving evidence does not settle the movement or self-capture rules.

## Scholarly reconstruction

The [Locus Ludi English rules publication](https://locusludi.ch/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/LUDUS-LATRUNCULORUM_rules_GB.pdf) supplies the Seneca reconstruction on pages 1–3. Page 2 illustrates rescue, corner custody, multiple prisoners in one move, and custody after a leap. The Seneca rules specify deployment, steps and friendly leaps, imprisonment followed by delayed removal, and opportunities for rescue. The source presents movement, starting arrangement, and the self-capture rule as reconstruction choices. The Piso section describes a different reconstruction.

The [Locus Ludi game index](https://locusludi.ch/play-ancient-online-games/) and [Ludii variant listing](https://ludii.games/variantDetails.php?keyword=Ludus+Latrunculorum&variant=1588) are secondary references. The supplied PDF was used as the primary source. The cloud proxy blocked the linked Notion specification, so the assignment brief supplied the remaining project requirements.
